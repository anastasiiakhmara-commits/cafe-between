The girl comes in at the twilight hour, when the shadows are growing long, quiet as a shadow herself. Her long, raven-black hair is wet, as is the back of her oversized white t-shirt; no jacket or coat despite the October chill, just the t-shirt, black leggings and black combat boots. She’s petite, but with eyes fierce enough to turn her into a presence. A monstera leaf seems to move out of her way all on its own, and something scurries away from her in the shadows.

The girl sits down at a high stool next to the bar; she has to pull herself up a little to climb there.

The innkeeper smiles and nods.

‘As usual?’ he asks.

She nods, a tiny bit of colour creeping up her cheeks.

He chooses a big mug, puts a spoonful of cocoa in it and pours hot milk over it; then, reaches up to the top shelf, gets a glass jar full of pink and white marshmallows, and carefully adds a spoonful on top of the drink.

The girl takes a sip, closes her eyes, and for a moment looks very young and quite tired.

‘I’ve heard there was some trouble with the Vodnik,’ the innkeeper says, watching her curiously.

The girl wrinkles her nose in distaste.

‘He claimed the canal, from the old bridge to the lake, as his territory, and would not let anyone enter without his permission.’

The innkeeper frowns, starting to say something, but she continues without a pause:

‘I had a whole embassy organized to petition him for the permissions for my people, as pompous as I could muster – with a carriage drawn by a pair of catfish, decorated with water-polished glass and waterweed streamers, with pompous speeches and gifts…’

‘And a certain rubber duck, as I’ve heard,’ the innkeeper interrupts with a wry smile.

She looks away and sighs.

‘News travels fast, I see. The duck… wasn’t my idea, but the girls decided that packaging it in a pearl-encrusted box and presenting it as a rare gift would be a perfect joke, and by the time I’ve discovered this it was too late to do anything but play along.’

The innkeeper keeps his expression carefully neutral, a hint of a smile hiding in the corners of his lips.

‘The Vodnik seemed to enjoy all the attention,’ she continues, ‘but the matter was dragging indefinitely. Well, you know how he is. Everything has to be put in ledgers, and then other ledgers have to be consulted, and then something the ledgers reference can’t be found or has to be changed, or the day is unfavourable for decisions, or the moon isn’t in the right phase.’

The innkeeper nods sympathetically.

‘And well. We would not confront him directly, but we did attempt to take measures.’

‘Painted water,’ he nods, looking amused.

The girl sighs.

‘That specific one wasn’t my invention, but yes. I still don’t know where the girls found so much paint, or why they decided to go with pink and purple. But it was… certainly memorable.

‘Also, an aspiring music band, entirely human and enthusiastic rather than skillful, was, let’s say, encouraged to practice at the banks. Young human men don’t need that much encouragement from my kind.

‘Then, there was that firework display.

‘And then, the toad infestation…’

‘And a certain bagiennik, an unusually stinky one, was reported to come upstream as I’ve heard,’ the innkeeper continues. ‘If I remember things right, he owed you a personal favor.’

The girl shrugs with a wry smile, not commenting.

‘However,’ the innkeeper says, ‘I can’t imagine the Vodnik giving in just like that? Especially since you seem to go to great lengths to keep those things untraceable to you directly?’

She smiles again.

‘We offered him to stand guard for him, to keep his domain free of… unfortunate mishaps. In exchange for those blasted passes, of course. He seemed pleased enough with the offer, even if he had started suspecting something.’

The innkeeper laughs quietly.

‘Mighty rusalka sentinels. That’s something I haven’t seen yet!’

Then, his expression becomes serious again.

‘But now you’ll have to keep your girls from bothering him – and I can imagine they might have enjoyed that a lot.’

The girl averts her eyes.

‘Well. I might have reminded them that the canal is technically on your territory. And that you might take things personally if the mischief continues.’

The innkeeper shakes his head.

‘So, now I am to be a boogeyman for overly enthusiastic young rusalki.’

She looks uncomfortable for the first time.

‘I hope I haven’t overstepped,’ she says, not quite apologetically.

He shrugs.

‘You have. But I would have probably done the same. I won’t ruin your game.’

She exhales, and sips her cocoa again. Then, she slides off the bar stool and squares her shoulders, instantly looking somewhat older and taller.

‘Won’t risk leaving younger sisters unsupervised any longer,’ she says, heading to the door.
