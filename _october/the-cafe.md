Golden October light is the first visitor in the cafe. It peers through the open window – the one facing the town square; the other one is shrouded in deep shadows, as usual. It sits on the worn wooden windowsill, gently touching the dark green leaves of an enormous monstera and a delicate maidenhair fern. It jumps on mismatched wooden chairs and over the long bar, glittering on glass jars filled with Chinese tea, ground coffee, mint, linden flowers, and, in one case, something that rather resembles dried beetle wings. It glistens over a pair of bronze and silver jugs, frustrated that it can’t get inside.

A tall, amber-eyed man at the bar raises his face to the light, unsquinting, as it layers red and amber highlights over his auburn hair, and reflects off a single silver streak at the temple. He’s making coffee, the slow way, heating an old bronze pot buried in sand; he looks at the tint of light on his hand and adds a pinch of nutmeg to the pot: something he always does for the first day of real autumn.

The second visitor is a yellow linden leaf landing softly on the table nearest to the window. The innkeeper picks it up and examines it closely; there are no linden trees in the square, but he is not surprised. He puts the leaf carefully on a shelf behind the bar, between a jar of cinnamon and a locked wooden box covered in intricate patterns.

The third visitor is a cat. It wanders in through the half-open door – the one facing the town square, obviously – and meows softly. The innkeeper nods to it as if it were a regular.

‘Quite warm for October, isn’t it,’ he says conversationally, pouring some water into a shallow clay bowl and setting it down next to the bar.

The cat drinks it and settles down in a pool of golden light, blinking slowly.

The man returns to his coffee, stirring it in precise, rehearsed motions. He’s looking out of the other window, the shadowy one, as if he’s waiting for something to move behind dark trees. There’s nothing to be seen, though; only willow branches rustling in the wind and pine trees seeming almost black where the golden light can’t reach.
